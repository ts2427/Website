Services

Where Research Meets Practice

Whether you need analytics infrastructure built, a research question answered with defensible methods, or counsel on governance and disclosure, Threshold Data Sciences brings the same rigor to consulting that peer review demands of research. I don't only advise on this — I study it.

Data Science & Analytics

Forecasting, data pipelines, executive dashboards, and reporting infrastructure that stakeholders actually use — from normalizing messy source data to production Python-and-Snowflake pipelines matched to the decisions they serve.

Research & Measurement

Study design, causal inference, and statistical analysis — answers you can defend, not just charts. Event studies, program evaluation, and measurement built to survive peer-review-grade scrutiny.

Governance, Risk & Compliance

Breach disclosure strategy, board oversight, and security spend prioritization, grounded in the empirical evidence on what regulation actually does inside organizations.

Why Threshold

Evidence, Operations, and Plain Language

Researcher First

Active empirical research on mandatory disclosure and cybersecurity governance, publications in the ISACA Journal, and peer-review service for international journals. The advice is built on evidence, not anecdote.

Twenty Years an Operator

Nearly two decades in telecom operations: building the reporting pipelines, sitting in the escalation calls, and watching compliance decisions get made under real pressure. The methods are academic; the questions are not.

Plain-Language Educator

A college instructor by trade. Findings arrive in language your board, your team, and your auditors can act on — the analysis is only as useful as the decision it enables.

How We Work

The Principal

About the Principal

Timothy D. Spivey
Principal & Founder
Timothy D. Spivey on LinkedIn Google Scholar Profile Research & Publications Threshold Effects (Blog)

Threshold Data Sciences is the consulting practice of Timothy D. Spivey — doctoral candidate (ABD) in Business Administration with a Business Analytics emphasis at the University of South Alabama, MBA in Business Analytics from the University of West Florida, and a management information systems supervisor with nearly twenty years in telecom operations.

His research examines what mandatory breach disclosure timing actually does — to markets, to organizations, and to boards. He has published in the ISACA Journal, serves as a peer reviewer for the Journal of Risk and Financial Management (MDPI) and Business Strategy & Development (Wiley), and teaches entrepreneurship and small business accounting at Pensacola State College.
